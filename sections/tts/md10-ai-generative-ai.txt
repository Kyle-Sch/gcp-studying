Section 10: AI and Generative AI.

Artificial intelligence and generative AI are among the fastest-growing areas of cloud computing. Before diving into the Google Cloud Platform services available, let's make sure the key terms are clear and well understood.

Generative AI refers to artificial intelligence that can create new content, such as text, images, audio, or structured data, based on a prompt you provide. An LLM, which stands for Large Language Model, is the foundation of most generative AI systems. These models are pre-trained on massive amounts of data and then fine-tuned for specific tasks. The word "large" in LLM refers to the number of parameters, which is the model's internal memory built up during training. Modern models can have billions or even trillions of parameters.

It's important to note that LLMs can hallucinate. Hallucination means the model produces output that sounds plausible and confident but is factually incorrect. This is a known limitation you should always keep in mind when working with generative AI. Moving on to prompting techniques, there are three you should know. Zero-shot prompting means you give the model a prompt with no examples at all. Few-shot prompting means you include a small number of examples in the prompt to guide the model's behavior. Chain-of-thought prompting means you explicitly ask the model to reason step by step before giving its final answer, which often improves accuracy on complex problems.

Now let's look at the Google Cloud AI services. The first and most central is Vertex AI, which is Google's unified platform for training, deploying, and managing machine learning models. Everything in the AI space on Google Cloud connects back to Vertex AI in some way. Within Vertex AI, Generative AI Studio is a user interface that lets you prototype and tune generative AI models without writing code. Model Garden is a browsable catalog where you can find and deploy both Google's own first-party models and third-party models.

AutoML is a service that allows you to train custom machine learning models on your own data without writing any training code. It handles the complexity of model selection and training automatically. Google also offers a set of pre-trained APIs for common tasks like computer vision, speech recognition, natural language processing, and language translation. These require no training at all and are ready to use immediately. Finally, Gemini in the Cloud Console is an AI assistant built directly into the Google Cloud management console, helping you navigate, query, and work with your cloud resources using natural language.

Let's close this section with prompt engineering best practices. When writing prompts for AI models, being explicit produces better results. Instead of saying "summarize this," you would say "summarize this in three bullet points." You should also define boundaries by telling the model exactly what to do and providing a fallback instruction for cases where it doesn't know the answer. Assigning a persona can also help. For example, telling the model "you are a GCP cloud architect" sets the context for the kind of response you expect. Keep your instructions short and clear by breaking complex directions into separate sentences. And always specify your desired output format, for example by saying "return this as a JSON array" or "format the response as a table." These practices dramatically improve the consistency and quality of model responses.
